The NDCG@40 Staircase

A plain-language walk through how a ranked list of 170 coins gets turned into one score — and why only the top 40 spots matter.

The one-sentence version

NDCG@40 asks a single question: of the 40 coins you put at the top of your list, how good did they actually turn out to be — and did you put the best ones highest? Everything else on this page just spells out how that question gets a number.

The name is a mouthful (Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain, at 40), but each piece is simple, and we'll take them one at a time: first the staircase, then the weights, then how they combine.

1 · Every coin stands on a step

Ten days after you submit, CrowdCent looks at what every coin actually did. But instead of using the raw returns (+12%, −4%, and so on), it lines all 170 coins up from worst to best and puts each one on its own step of a staircase.

The staircase runs from just above 0 to exactly 1. With 170 coins there are 170 steps, so each step is 1 ÷ 170 = 0.0059 taller than the one below it. The best-performing coin stands on step 1.000, the second best on 0.994, the third on 0.988, all the way down to the worst coin on 0.006.

Hover a step to see its exact height. Every step is the same size — the staircase never has a big jump or a flat spot.

This is the key thing to notice: the shape of the staircase is identical every single day. Whether the whole crypto market soared or crashed, there is always exactly one coin on step 1.000, one on step 0.994, and so on. The only thing that changes from day to day is which coin is standing on which step.

Your model's entire job is to guess that assignment ahead of time.

2 · How many coins fit in each band?

Because the steps are evenly spaced, the same number of coins always land in each percentage band. Slice the staircase into ten bands of 10% each, and there are 17 coins in every band — never more, never fewer.

Coins per 10% band

170 coins ÷ 10 bands = 17 coins in every band, every day.

Bands where the top 40 liveBands that don't count toward NDCG@40

The top 40 coins are 40 ÷ 170 = 23.5% of the field, so they fill the top two bands (34 coins) plus 6 coins from the third.

Slice finer, into 5% bands, and you get 8 or 9 coins per band (170 ÷ 20 = 8.5, so the bands alternate). Either way, the point is the same — there is a fixed number of seats at every level, and being "in the top 5%" means being one of about 8 coins.

3 · The weights: the top spot is worth the most

Now flip to your list. You submitted 170 coins in the order you thought they'd perform. The score only looks at your first 40, and it doesn't treat those 40 equally. Spot #1 counts the most, spot #2 a bit less, and so on down to spot #40, which counts the least.

The weight for each spot comes from a small formula: 1 divided by log2(spot + 1). You don't need to know what a log is to use the table — the "+1" is only there so that spot #1 doesn't divide by zero. What matters is the shape: the weight starts at exactly 1.0 and shrinks quickly at first, then slowly.

How much each of the 40 spots counts

Weight = 1 ÷ log2(spot + 1). Spot #1 is worth about 5× spot #40.

The full weight table, spots 1 – 40

Highlighted: the three spots used in the worked example below.

Read it like this: a coin you put at #1 has its step height counted in full. A coin you put at #3 has its step height counted at half. A coin at #40 has its step height counted at less than a fifth. And a coin you put at #41 or lower? It's not counted at all.

4 · Putting them together

Here's the whole calculation, in words:

Walk down your list from spot #1 to spot #40. At each spot, look up which step the coin actually ended up on, multiply that step height by the spot's weight, and add it to a running total.

That running total is the "cumulative gain." Two things make it big: putting coins that land on high steps into your top 40 at all, and putting the very highest of them in the top few spots where the weights are biggest.

  • You had kPEPE and kSHIB the wrong way round. kPEPE was the better coin but sat in the #2 spot with the smaller weight.

1.595 ÷ 1.619 = 0.985 you captured 98.5% of what was possible with those three coins

The 1.5% you lost is entirely the kPEPE/kSHIB swap. For the real competition the same thing happens with 40 spots instead of 3, and the "perfect" total is always the same number — about 10.09 — because the top 40 steps of the staircase are the same every day.

5 · Three things this means for you

A random list doesn't score zero

Shuffle the 170 coins at random and your top 40 will still contain some genuinely good coins by luck, each contributing its step height. That's why CrowdCent says a random submission scores about 0.55, not 0. Think of 0.55 as the floor, and judge a model by how far above it lands.

Who's in your top 40 matters more than their exact order

Putting a step-0.2 coin into your top 40 wastes a slot that could have held a step-0.9 coin — that costs far more than having two good coins in swapped positions. Get the membership right first; the ordering within the 40 is the fine-tuning.

The middle of your list is invisible

Whether you ranked a coin 80th or 110th makes no difference to NDCG@40. Only the top 40 spots are looked at. (CrowdCent also runs the same calculation on your bottom 40 — the coins you'd short — and averages the two, but that's the same idea flipped upside down.)
